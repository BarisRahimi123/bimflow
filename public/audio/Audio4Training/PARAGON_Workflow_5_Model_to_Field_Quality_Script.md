# PARAGON BIM Workflow — Part 5: Model-to-Field Quality
## Workflow 8.1 — Training Script for ElevenLabs

### [INTRO — Friendly, Welcoming Tone]

Welcome to the final lesson in our PARAGON BIM workflow series.

So far, everything's been about getting the model right and approved. But construction happens in the real world, and sometimes the model and the field don't match. Maybe something was installed differently, or a field condition turned out not to match the model. This workflow is about what you do when that happens — the model-to-field quality process.

Quick orientation first: this chart is organized into lanes, one per team, because the whole point here is knowing who hands off to whom. Let me introduce the players, then walk the flow.

---

### [SECTION 1 — Who's Involved]

There are six lanes on this chart, and each is a role. Let me name them so the handoffs make sense.

There's Trade BIM — the trade's modeling side. There's the Hoffman C-M, the construction manager. There's Hoffman Quality — the Q-A-Q-C team. There's Hoffman BIM. There's Applied Materials, the owner. And there's Trade Field — the trade's boots-on-the-ground crew.

Quick vocabulary from the legend: Q-A-Q-C means Quality Assurance and Quality Control. BIM is the 3D building model. And I-F-F, which you know by now, is the Issued For Fabrication status. Keep those six lanes in mind — the story is really about an issue traveling between them.

---

### [SECTION 2 — Spotting and Reporting the Issue]

It begins when a model-to-field quality issue is identified. And notice — it can be spotted by almost anyone in any lane. The trade, the field crew, quality, the owner — any of them might be the first to catch that something doesn't line up. Quality is everyone's job here.

Whoever finds it, the next move is the same: communicate the issue to Hoffman Quality and the C-M. You don't sit on it, and you don't quietly fix it yourself — you report it up so it gets tracked and handled properly.

---

### [SECTION 3 — Agreeing on a Resolution]

Once it's reported, the issue gets assigned to a responsible party, along with a proposed resolution. Somebody owns it, and there's a first idea for how to fix it.

Then comes a collaborative step that I want to highlight: Hoffman Quality, the Hoffman C-M, and the Trade Field crew develop and agree upon the resolution together. This isn't one person deciding in isolation — the office and the field agree on the fix as a team. That agreement is what makes the resolution stick.

---

### [SECTION 4 — The Big Fork: Model or Field?]

Here's the central decision of the whole workflow. Is the fix a model adjustment, or a field adjustment? In other words — do we change the model to match reality, or do we change what's built in the field? The path splits here.

Let's take the MODEL path first. If the right answer is to fix the model: you make the model resolution, you document it on Newforma Konekt, and — this is the important part — you resubmit it for I-F-F approval. Why? Because you changed the model, so it has to go back through that fabrication-approval gate from the last lesson. There's a timing target noted here too: that model resolution loop is expected to turn around in about two weeks. Then Hoffman reviews, approves, and closes the Newforma Konekt issue.

Now the FIELD path. If the right answer is to adjust the physical work: the Hoffman Q-A-Q-C team determines the appropriate resolution, and the field adjustment gets performed. Then there's one more question on this path, and it's a big one.

---

### [SECTION 5 — Gating vs. Non-Gating, and the Green Tag]

On the field path, you have to ask: is this a gating constraint for a green tag?

Let me unpack that. A green tag is the sign-off that says this work is good and accepted. "Gating" means the issue is blocking that green tag — the work cannot be signed off until this is resolved. "Non-gating" means it's not a blocker — it should still be fixed, but it isn't holding up acceptance.

That distinction drives urgency, and the chart actually attaches timeframes to it. A gating issue gets a much tighter turnaround — on the order of one week — because it's holding everything up. A non-gating issue has more room, closer to four weeks. So gating equals urgent; non-gating equals important but not blocking.

---

### [SECTION 6 — Closing It Out]

However the issue gets resolved — model or field, gating or not — it ends the same way. Completion is communicated to Hoffman, and then the issue is reviewed, approved, and closed in Procore.

That final close-out in Procore is what makes it official. The problem isn't just fixed — it's documented, signed off, and on the record. And that's the whole point of this workflow: not just to correct the mismatch, but to make sure every correction is agreed, tracked, and formally closed.

---

### [OUTRO]

And that completes the model-to-field quality workflow — and our entire PARAGON BIM workflow series.

Let's zoom out one last time. You learned how design gets delivered, how the federated model is built, how clashes get coordinated and resolved, how models get approved for fabrication, and now how quality issues between the model and the field get handled. That's the full life of a project, start to finish.

You've got the whole picture now. Great work making it through all five — go put it to use.
