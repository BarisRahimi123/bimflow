# PARAGON BIM Workflow — Part 3: Collaboration & Clash Resolution
## Workflow 6.3 — Training Script for ElevenLabs

### [INTRO — Friendly, Welcoming Tone]

Welcome back! This is part three of our PARAGON BIM workflow series — and this is the big one. This is the heart of coordination: how clashes get found, and how issues get resolved.

This chart has the most decision points of any in the series, so I'm going to walk you through it as a series of simple questions. At each step, you ask yourself one question, and the answer tells you where to go next. Follow the questions, and the whole thing flows. Let's get into it.

---

### [SECTION 1 — Finding the Clash]

It starts with clash detection. You take your scope and you clash it against two things: the design model, and the other trades' construction models. You're checking — does my work physically conflict with anyone else's?

You run your clash reports and you adjudicate them — that's a fancy word for reviewing each clash and deciding whether it's a real problem or just noise. When you confirm a real clash, you create a Newforma Konekt issue ticket. That ticket is how the problem gets tracked from here on out. No ticket, no tracking — so this step matters.

---

### [SECTION 2 — Question One: Can I Fix It Myself?]

Now the first decision. Ask yourself: is this self-resolvable?

In other words — can I fix this on my own, just by adjusting my own model, without needing anyone else?

If yes — great. You fix the issue, you update your construction model, and you're essentially done with that one. Easy path.

If no — it means other people need to be involved, and we move to the next question.

---

### [SECTION 3 — Question Two: Can We Settle It Without a Meeting?]

So the clash needs coordination with others. Next question: is this resolvable outside of a meeting?

Sometimes a quick back-and-forth in the Newforma Konekt ticket is enough — a couple of comments, everyone agrees, done. That's resolving it outside a meeting. You review and adjudicate the issue right there in the ticket.

But if it's tangled — if it really needs people in a room talking it through — then it goes to a coordination meeting, where it gets adjudicated together. Either way, the goal is the same: reach an agreed resolution.

---

### [SECTION 4 — Question Three: Minor or Major?]

Here's the most important decision in the entire workflow. Once you understand the issue, you classify it: is this a MINOR deviation from the design, or a MAJOR one? This determines everything that happens next, so let me make the difference clear.

It's a MINOR deviation if: it doesn't impact multiple disciplines, it doesn't require the design team to do new design work through a D-R-B or R-F-I, it's not a significant change from the basis of design, it doesn't significantly change project cost, and — critically — it stays within tolerances. For the exact rules, you check the H-C-C C-P-E-P, Appendix 6.1, type 2, and the Jacobs A-9 document. Minor means: small, contained, within the rules.

It's a MAJOR deviation if: it impacts multiple disciplines, it requires design input issued through a D-R-B or R-F-I, it's a significant change from the basis of design, it significantly changes cost, there's a clash within or between the contract documents themselves, or it exceeds tolerances. For those rules, you check the same C-P-E-P Appendix 6.1, but type 1, and again the Jacobs A-9 document. Major means: big, far-reaching, outside the rules — the design team has to weigh in.

So before you do anything else, decide: minor or major? That single call sets your path.

---

### [SECTION 5 — Question Four: Is an R-F-I Required?]

Flowing from that classification comes the next question: is an R-F-I required?

If yes — you write the R-F-I. And notice where that points you: to workflow 7.1, the I-F-F submittal procedure, which connects this whole process to getting your model formally approved. Writing the R-F-I gets the design team's official answer.

If no R-F-I is needed — you simply fix the issue and update your construction model, just like the easy path from before.

---

### [SECTION 6 — Closing the Loop]

Final question: is the issue actually resolved?

If no — the loop isn't done. You update the Newforma Konekt issue and keep working it until it's truly closed out. Don't abandon a ticket halfway.

If yes — three things happen to lock it in. You update your construction model with the fix. You start the I-F-F submittal procedure, which we'll cover in the next lesson. And you update the federated model so everyone sees the resolved version.

And that's the rhythm of coordination: find the clash, log the ticket, and then walk the questions — can I fix it alone, can we settle it without a meeting, is it minor or major, does it need an R-F-I, and is it resolved? Answer those honestly and every clash has a clear path home.

---
