### [INTRO — Friendly, Welcoming Tone]

Welcome back! This is part two of our PARAGON BIM workflow series.

In part one, you learned how the design gets delivered and how you publish your construction model into the federated model. Today, we crack that federated model open and look at what's actually inside it.

This one's shorter, but it's foundational. Once you understand the layers of the federated model, every coordination conversation you have will make a lot more sense. Let's take a look.

---

### [SECTION 1 — What the Federated Model Is]

First, the concept. The Federated Model — the F-M — is the master combined model. It's not one model that somebody drew; it's a stack of many models, layered together, so the whole team can see how everything fits in one place.

Think of it like a transparency stack — design on one layer, construction on another, neighboring projects on another — all lined up in the same coordinate space. When you open the F-M, you're looking at all of those at once.

So what are the layers? There are four main types of files you'll find in there. Let me walk through each one.

---

### [SECTION 2 — The Design Model Layers]

The first two layers come from the design team. And the difference between them is really important.

Layer one is the Design Model Issued — you'll see it tagged "underscore ISSUED." This is the official, released design. It's the version that's locked in and ready for you to coordinate and build against. When it says Issued, it's real.

Layer two is the Design Model W-I-P — tagged "underscore WIP," which stands for Work In Progress. This is design that isn't final yet. It's included to give you early context — a heads-up about what's coming — so you can start coordinating against it before it's officially released.

Here's the key rule about the W-I-P layer: it's temporary. As the design firms up and that scope gets officially issued, the W-I-P version gets removed and replaced by the issued version. The W-I-P may also stick around as reference information for R-F-Is. So treat W-I-P as "preview, subject to change" — never as final truth.

---

### [SECTION 3 — The Construction and Coordination Layers]

The next two layers are about construction.

Layer three is the Construction Model — the C-M. This is the work the trades build, including yours. Remember from part one — when you publish your C-M, this is the layer it lands in. It's the detailed, real-world version of the design.

Layer four is Inter-project Coordination Models. On a big campus, your project sits next to other projects. These files let you pull in models from neighboring projects, as needed, so you can coordinate across project boundaries and avoid clashing with something just outside your own scope.

So those are your four layers: Issued design, W-I-P design for context, the construction models, and neighboring-project models for cross-coordination.

---

### [OUTRO]

That's the federated model, layer by layer. Issued design is the truth, W-I-P is a temporary preview, construction models are what the trades build, and inter-project models keep you coordinated with your neighbors.

Now that you know what's inside the model, you're ready for the lesson where it all gets used — coordination and clash resolution. That's next. See you there.
