Hey there! Welcome back to our BIM training series.

In this module, we're going to talk about one of the most important documents you'll use on this project - the A-7 LOD Matrix.

LOD stands for Level of Development. And this document tells you exactly what should be in your model, what should NOT be in your model, and what information you need to include for each element.

Think of the A-7 as your modeling checklist. Before you model anything, you should be asking yourself: Does the A-7 say I need to include this? And if so, what metadata do I need to attach to it?

Let's break it down.

The A-7 is organized by discipline. You'll find separate sections for Process, Mechanical, Electrical, Structural, Architectural, Fire Protection, Telecom, Instrumentation, and more.

Each section tells you three things:

First, what elements to INCLUDE in your model.

Second, what elements to EXCLUDE from your model.

And third, what metadata or parameters are REQUIRED for each element.

Let's look at a real example. Say you're modeling process piping.

The A-7 tells us that process piping 2 inches and larger must be included in the model.

But piping smaller than 2 inches? That's generally excluded - unless it's a designated stress line or has some special requirement.

So if you're looking at a half-inch line on the P and ID, don't automatically model it. Check the A-7 first.

Now let's talk about metadata. This is where a lot of people make mistakes.

It's not enough to just draw a pipe in your model. You need to include the required parameters.

For process piping, the A-7 typically requires:
Name,
Line Number,
Size - and that's nominal size,
Length,
Layer,
and Package Name.

If your model is missing any of these parameters, it's not complete. It won't pass QA review.

Let's look at equipment as another example.

The A-7 tells us that tagged equipment permanently attached to a foundation should be included. But it's modeled as a volumetric representation only - meaning you show the overall shape and size, not every little detail inside the equipment.

Points of connection must be shown. That's where pipes connect to the equipment.

Equipment supports like lugs, legs, and skirts are shown as spatial representations only.

And the required metadata typically includes TAG ID, Description, Layer, and Package Name.

Here's an important distinction. The A-7 tells you what level of DETAIL to show - not what level of ACCURACY.

Detail is about how much geometry you model. Accuracy is about whether it's in the right location. Those are different things, and they're covered by different documents.

The A-7 covers detail. The A-9 covers accuracy and tolerances. We'll talk about A-9 in another module.

Now, let's talk about exclusions. The A-7 also tells you what NOT to model.

For example, in process piping, you typically exclude:
Piping designated to be field routed,
Valve handle swing direction,
Test ports, vents, and drains that are only for construction purposes,
And containment pump discharge lines 2 inches and smaller.

Why does this matter? Because if you model things that should be excluded, you're wasting time. And you might create false clashes during coordination.

So before you model something, always ask: Does the A-7 say to include this or exclude this?

Let me give you a practical tip. When you start working on a new area or system, pull up the A-7 and find your discipline section. Read through it before you start modeling. Understand what's expected.

Then, as you model, keep the A-7 open as a reference. Check it whenever you're unsure about whether to include something.

Here's another tip. The A-7 is organized in a table format. Each row is an element type. The columns tell you whether to include it, what detail to show, what to exclude, and what metadata is required.

Get familiar with this format. Once you understand how to read it, you can quickly look up any element.

Let's recap the key points.

Number one: The A-7 LOD Matrix defines what goes in your model and what stays out.

Number two: It's organized by discipline. Find your section and study it.

Number three: For each element, know what's INCLUDED, what's EXCLUDED, and what METADATA is required.

Number four: Size thresholds matter. For example, process piping 2 inches and above is modeled. Below 2 inches is typically not.

Number five: Include all required metadata. A model without the right parameters is not complete.

Number six: Don't model things that should be excluded. It wastes time and creates false clashes.

That's the A-7 LOD Matrix. It's your guide to what belongs in the model.
