# Clash Reporting for IFF — Part 2: Running the Review
## Clash Report Training, Part 2 — Training Script for ElevenLabs

> Bracketed headers are screen cues only — for syncing the software screenshots. Paste ONLY the narration text underneath into ElevenLabs.

---

### [INTRO — Friendly, Welcoming Tone]

Welcome back! This is part two of our clash reporting series.

In part one, you got everything set up — your grey, yellow, and red sets, and a clash report ready to run. Today's the fun part: actually working through the clashes and deciding what's a real problem and what's just noise.

This is where judgment comes in. The software finds the conflicts, but you decide what they mean. Let me show you the most efficient way to work through them. Let's go.

---

### [SECTION 1 — The Two Buckets]

Here's the core idea of the whole review. As you go through clashes, every single one lands in one of two buckets.

Bucket one is "Approved Non-Issues" — clashes that aren't actually problems. The model shows an overlap, but in reality it's fine.

Bucket two is "Actual Issues" — real conflicts that someone needs to fix.

That's the entire job, boiled down: look at each clash and ask, "real problem, or not?" Non-issue or actual issue. Everything else is just method.

---

### [SECTION 2 — The Efficient Way to Work]

Now let me give you the workflow that saves you the most time, because the slow way will wear you out.

Start with the first clash batch at the top and group all the clashes into one group. Then go through them, analyzing each one and creating viewpoint notes as you sort them into your two buckets.

Here's the efficiency trick: for the Actual Issues, make the viewpoint — but don't bother naming them yet. Just get really zoomed in, tight on the clash, so there's no question about what you're looking at. Save all the naming for the very end.

Why? Because constantly jumping out to set the view, name the issue, then jumping back in — over and over — is exhausting and slow. Capture all the views first in one pass, name them all in a second pass. Two clean passes beats a hundred little interruptions.

---

### [SECTION 3 — Using the Trade's Own Viewpoints]

Here's a smart move if the trade gave you something to work with. If the trade included their own viewpoints when they made their I-F-F request, import those into your N-W-F.

Why bother? Because those viewpoints jump you straight to every issue the trade already knows about. That means you won't waste time re-logging an issue they already flagged — no duplicates, no stepping on each other.

One honest technical note from the guide: you can pull viewpoints from BIM Track too, but BIM Track doesn't show the markups when you click "View in Model." So importing into your N-W-F directly is the better route when you've got the option.

Then you simply repeat this same process for every clash batch. Same rhythm, batch after batch.

---

### [SECTION 4 — The Self-Clash Check]

Now a step people forget — and it's important. So far you've been clashing the trade against everyone else. But you also need to clash the trade against itself.

Here's how. Go back to the Subcontractor versus C-M M-E-P report and open the Rules tab. Uncheck the rule called "Items in the same file." Unchecking that is what lets the report catch a trade clashing with its own work — their own pipe into their own tray, that kind of thing.

When you group these results, give the folder its own name: "Approved Non-Issues — Self." And keep it separate from your regular Approved Non-Issues. That separation matters, because self-clashes are a different conversation than clashes between trades — you don't want them mixed together.

---

### [SECTION 5 — Don't Trust the Sort Order]

A quick reality check about what you're seeing. A lot of these clashes are going to be "noise clashes" — overlaps that aren't real problems at all. That's normal.

Now, the report does try to help: it sorts based on the severity of the two intersecting objects, so the real problems often show up near the top. That's useful — but here's the warning: do not rely on it. The sort is a hint, not a guarantee. A real issue can absolutely be buried further down. So verify everything. Don't let a tidy sort order talk you into skipping the bottom of the list.

---

### [SECTION 6 — Naming, Uploading, and Responding]

You've reviewed everything — now let's package it up. Remember those Actual Issues you left unnamed? Now's the time to name them, and there's a required format. It's the same SUB-versus-SUB convention you already know.

You write the responsible trade, "versus," the other trade, a dash, and then a plain description. For example: "O-E-G versus H-M-C — cable tray and pipe valve clashing." And for a self-clash, it's the same trade on both sides — "O-E-G versus O-E-G — your own tray is into your conduits." Keeping this format consistent is what lets Hoffman and every trade read each other's issues instantly.

Then three final steps. Create a folder in the Saved Viewpoints dialog and label it with the I-F-F name. Drop all your issues in that folder and upload it to BIM Track. And finally, upload your viewpoint notes to Procore as a supplement, and respond to the I-F-F request there.

And that closes the review — sorted, named, uploaded to BIM Track, and answered on Procore.

---

### [OUTRO]

Nice work. You've run a full clash review: two buckets, an efficient two-pass method, the self-clash check, a healthy distrust of the sort order, and clean naming and upload at the end.

But sometimes one review isn't the end of the story — the trade fixes things and resubmits, and you have to look again. That second look has its own careful process, and that's part three. See you there.

---

*End of script.*
