# PARAGON BIM Workflow — Part 1: Design Delivery

### [INTRO — Friendly, Welcoming Tone]

Hey there, and welcome! This is part one of our walkthrough of the PARAGON BIM workflows.

Now, I know — you're looking at a flowchart full of boxes and arrows, and it can feel intimidating. Don't worry. My job is to walk you through the story these charts are telling, step by step, so the process makes sense in your head. Forget the boxes for a second and just follow the journey.

There are five workflows in this series. Part one — today — is Design Delivery: how the design shows up and becomes something you can build from. Then we'll cover how the federated model is structured, how coordination and clash resolution works, how you get a model approved for fabrication, and finally how quality issues get handled in the field.

Let's start at the very beginning — where the design comes from.

---

### [SECTION 1 — The Two Pipelines]

Before we follow the steps, here's the most important thing to understand about this whole project. Information reaches you through two separate pipelines, and you need to know which is which.

Pipeline one is B-I-M 360 — that's for model files. The actual 3D models live here.

Pipeline two is Procore — that's for the documents. Drawings, specs, and R-F-I responses come through Procore.

So model files through B-I-M 360, paperwork through Procore. Keep those two straight and you'll always know where to look.

---

### [SECTION 2 — The Design Arrives]

Okay, here's how it begins. The design team, Jacobs, transmits their design models to everyone through B-I-M 360. That's the starting gun.

Now it's your turn. The first thing you do is analyze the package and isolate your scope. In plain terms: out of everything in that release, figure out which parts are actually yours to build. You're filtering the big picture down to your lane.

From there, the path splits based on the situation. Either you're modeling brand-new initial scope — work you haven't touched yet — or you're incorporating a package change, which is an update to something that already existed, often driven by an R-F-I. New work or revised work — either way, it flows into the same next step.

---

### [SECTION 3 — Building and Publishing]

Now you do the real work: you create or update your Construction Models — we call those C-Ms. This is your detailed, build-it-for-real version of the design.

Once your construction model is updated, it gets pushed up into the Federated Model — the F-M — which is the big combined model where everyone's work comes together. Publishing your C-M into the F-M is how your work becomes visible to the whole team for coordination.

And finally, you generate a package transmittal, or notification, so everyone knows a new or updated package is available. That closes the loop — the design came in, you scoped it, modeled it, published it, and announced it.

---

### [SECTION 4 — Where R-F-Is Fit In]

One more piece to understand. Sometimes you hit a question that needs the design team to answer — that's an R-F-I, a Request For Information.

When the answer to an R-F-I includes updated models, those come back to you through Procore as R-F-I models. And that loops you right back to analyzing the package and isolating scope, because now you've got new information to fold into your construction model. So the process isn't always a straight line — R-F-Is feed answers back in, and you re-incorporate them.

---

### [OUTRO]

So that's design delivery in a nutshell. Jacobs sends models through B-I-M 360, documents come through Procore, you isolate your scope, build your construction model, publish it into the federated model, and notify the team.

In the next lesson, we'll open up that federated model and see exactly what's inside it — because understanding its layers makes everything else click. See you there.
