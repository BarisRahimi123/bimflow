Hey there! Welcome to this module on the A-11 Deliverables Format Table.

This document answers a simple but crucial question: Is this deliverable coming to me in 2D or 3D?

Why does this matter? Because it directly affects how you build your construction model.

If something is delivered in 3D, you have a model to reference. You can see the geometry, the routing, the spatial relationships.

But if something is delivered in 2D only, you have drawings and documents - but no 3D model. That means YOU have to interpret those 2D documents and create the 3D geometry yourself.

This is a big deal for planning your work. If you're expecting a 3D model but only get 2D drawings, you'll need more time and effort to complete your scope.

Let's walk through how the A-11 is structured.

The A-11 is organized by discipline and deliverable type. For each deliverable, it tells you the format: either 2D, which typically means PDF, or 3D, which typically means Navisworks NWD format.

Let me give you some examples.

P and IDs - Piping and Instrumentation Diagrams - are delivered in 2D as PDF documents.

This makes sense when you think about it. A P and ID is a schematic. It shows the process flow, the equipment, the instruments, the line numbers. But it's not spatially accurate. It doesn't show you exactly where things are located in the building.

So when you're modeling piping, you'll use the P and ID to understand WHAT needs to be connected, but you'll use other documents to understand WHERE it goes.

Piping plans and sections, on the other hand, are delivered in 3D as Navisworks models.

This gives you the spatial layout. You can see where the pipes are routed, how they connect to equipment, and how they relate to other systems.

But remember - as we discussed in earlier modules - these design models are not at the full level of detail you need for construction. You'll still need to add detail based on specs and submittals.

General Arrangement plans for equipment and devices are delivered in 2D.

These show you equipment locations on a floor plan. You'll use these to understand where major equipment is positioned.

Specifications are delivered as 2D documents - typically PDFs.

Details are also 2D.

Distribution schematics and circuiting diagrams are 2D.

See the pattern? Documents that are schematic or informational in nature tend to be 2D. Documents that need to show spatial relationships tend to be 3D.

Now, here's where the A-11 becomes really valuable for planning.

Before you start modeling a system or area, look at the A-11. Identify all the deliverables that apply to your scope. Note which ones are 2D and which are 3D.

For the 3D deliverables, you'll have geometry to reference.

For the 2D deliverables, you'll need to interpret the information and build the geometry yourself.

This helps you estimate effort more accurately. More 2D-only scope means more modeling work for you.

Let me share another important point. The A-11 also includes a note about dimensions and verification.

For some deliverables, you'll see guidance that says "Dimensions taken from model, contractor verifies based on final equipment."

What does this mean? It means the dimensions shown are based on the design model, but YOU are responsible for verifying them against the actual equipment you're installing.

If you receive a pump that's slightly different from what was assumed in design, you need to model YOUR pump - the real one from your submittal - not just copy the design model.

This ties back to what we discussed in module one. You're creating a Construction Model based on YOUR approved submittals. The design gives you the intent. You provide the constructible detail.

Let's talk about how to use the A-11 in practice.

Step one: Find your discipline section in the A-11.

Step two: List out all the deliverables that apply to your scope.

Step three: Note the format for each - 2D or 3D.

Step four: For 2D deliverables, plan additional time to interpret and model.

Step five: For 3D deliverables, understand that you still need to add detail and verify against submittals.

Here's a quick reference for common deliverables:

P and IDs - 2D
Piping plans and sections - 3D
Equipment plans - 2D
Specifications - 2D
Details - 2D
Distribution schematics - 2D
Circuiting diagrams - 2D

Of course, always check the actual A-11 for your project, as deliverables may vary.

One more thing. The A-11 helps you understand the document hierarchy.

When you're building your construction model, you're pulling information from multiple sources: 3D design models, 2D drawings, specifications, details, P and IDs.

If these sources conflict, you need to know which one takes priority. The order of precedence is typically:

Specifications take priority over everything.
Then P and IDs.
Then 2D IFC drawings.
Then 3D models.
Then other 2D drawings.

This is documented in the BIM execution requirements. But the A-11 helps you understand what format each source is in, so you know what you're working with.

Alright, let's wrap up with the key takeaways.

Number one: The A-11 tells you whether each deliverable is 2D or 3D format.

Number two: 3D deliverables give you geometry to reference. 2D deliverables require you to interpret and create geometry.

Number three: More 2D scope means more modeling effort. Plan accordingly.

Number four: Even with 3D deliverables, you still need to add detail and verify against your submittals.

Number five: Understand the document hierarchy. Specs beat P and IDs beat drawings beat models.

Number six: Use the A-11 to plan your work before you start modeling.
