# Newforma Konekt — Part 4: Design Issues & Questions
## The Design-Team Workflow — Training Script for ElevenLabs

> Bracketed headers are screen cues only — for syncing visuals. Paste ONLY the narration text underneath into ElevenLabs.

---

### [INTRO — Friendly, Welcoming Tone]

Welcome to the final lesson in our Newforma Konekt series.

Quick heads-up before we start: this lesson is a bit more specialized. Parts one through three were for everyone. This one centers on the Design Issue and Question workflow — and the responding side is primarily for the design team, Jacobs. If you're a subcontractor, you'll still want to understand the whole loop, because you start it and you finish it. So stick with me — knowing the full cycle helps everybody.

This workflow is for design questions that sit below the formal R-F-I threshold — the kind of thing handled per the Jacobs A-9 Change Management rules. Let's walk the whole journey.

---

### [SECTION 1 — The Three-Step Sequence]

Before any buttons, let's understand the flow. There are three steps, and three players: the subcontractor, the Hoffman C-M, and Jacobs.

Step one — the subcontractor raises it. They create an issue, set the type to bracket J-E-G, Design Issues slash Questions, and assign it to their Hoffman C-M. The Hoffman C-M reviews it and forwards it on to the right Jacobs person — a Space Manager or a Discipline Engineer.

Step two — Jacobs responds. They review the question, provide their answer, and send it back by changing the status from OPEN to "Jacobs Response Provided."

Step three — the subcontractor closes the loop. They review the response and put the solution into action. If the answer carries a cost or schedule impact, or if it was requested, they write a confirming R-F-I. Then they change the status to RECONCILED — and the issue is done.

So: subcontractor asks, Jacobs answers, subcontractor reconciles. Keep that triangle in your head and the rest is just clicks.

---

### [SECTION 2 — Finding Design Issues on the Web]

Let's start with how the design team finds the issues waiting for them, using the web platform.

Open the Issues viewer. Click the Quick Filter button to see the saved filter presets. In that flyout, pick the one named star, bracket J-E-G, Design Issues slash Questions — Open. That instantly narrows the list to open design questions.

Now click the Filter button to fine-tune. In the "Assigned to" field, select your name — or leave it blank if you want to see everything. In the "Zone" field, pick the level or sector where you have scope. Then click Apply.

And there's your focused list — just the open design questions in your area. No scrolling through everyone else's work.

---

### [SECTION 3 — Finding Design Issues in Navisworks]

You can do the same thing right inside Navisworks, which is great when you want to see the issue in 3D context.

With your model open, click the Newforma Konekt tab, then click the Konekt icon to open the side pane. Select "paragon" from the Hub dropdown, and pick your building from the Project dropdown.

From there it mirrors the web. Click Filter, then Quick Filter, and choose the preset — in Navisworks it's star, bracket J-E-G, Design Issue Post I-F-C — Open. Set your name in the Assign To field, set your Zone, and click Apply.

Same result as the web, but now each issue ties directly to what you're seeing in the model.

---

### [SECTION 4 — Responding on the Web]

Okay, you've found an issue and you're ready to answer it. Here's the response workflow on the web.

Click the issue to open it. Click the three dots and choose "Open Details Page" for the full view. Go to the Comments tab, type your response, and click Post. If you have a drawing, sketch, or markup to share, add it under the Attachments tab — that step's optional.

Now the important fields. Change the Status to "02, Design Response Provided" — that's the flag that tells everyone you've answered. Then change the "Assigned to" field back to the trade who originally sent it. Not sure who that was? Check the History tab; it shows the full trail. Finally, click Save.

One more thing to watch for: if your response means the design model itself needs to change, set the "D-M Model Update" field to "00, Needed." That signals a model revision is coming.

---

### [SECTION 5 — Responding in Navisworks]

If you'd rather stay in Navisworks, the response steps are nearly identical.

Click View slash Edit on the issue. Add your response in the Comments tab and click Post. Add attachments if you need to — optional. Change the Status to "02, Design Response Provided." Change the "Assigned to" field back to the trade who sent it, checking the History tab if you're unsure. And click Save.

And just like on the web: if the design model needs revising because of your answer, set "D-M Model Update" to "00, Needed." Same rule, same flag, whichever tool you're in.
