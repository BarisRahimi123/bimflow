# PARAGON BIM Workflow — Part 4: IFF Submittal Procedure
## Workflow 7.1 — Training Script for ElevenLabs

> Bracketed headers are screen cues only — for syncing the flowchart on screen. Paste ONLY the narration text underneath into ElevenLabs.

---

### [INTRO — Friendly, Welcoming Tone]

Welcome back! This is part four of our PARAGON BIM workflow series.

In the last lesson, you resolved your clashes and got your model coordinated. So now the question is: how do you get that model officially approved so you can actually fabricate from it? That's what I-F-F means — Issued For Fabrication. It's the green light to start building real, physical parts from your model.

This workflow is basically a two-stage approval gate with a few off-ramps. Let me walk you through it.

---

### [SECTION 1 — It Starts in the Weekly Meeting]

Before anything gets submitted, there's preparation. Every week, in the subcontractor meetings, the team reviews upcoming I-F-Fs and package releases together.

What do they talk about? The schedule, any open D-R-Bs and R-F-Is, the Newforma Konekt issues, constructability concerns, and whether anything needs to be re-issued for fabrication because of a design change. So nothing gets submitted as a surprise — it's all previewed in the weekly meeting first. That's your chance to flag problems early, before the clock starts.

Once that prep is done and the model is clean, you're ready for I-F-F submittal.

---

### [SECTION 2 — Gate One: The CM Review]

Here's the first gate. Your submittal goes to the Hoffman C-M — the construction manager — for review. And one helpful note: it's optional but recommended that you, the subcontractor, be included in this review. Take that offer when you can; being in the room means you hear concerns firsthand.

Now the first decision: does it pass the C-M review?

If no — it doesn't pass — you head down the correction path. You ask: is there a schedule impact? If yes, you submit a Schedule Change Notice — an S-C-N — to the Hoffman C-M and Hoffman BIM, so everyone knows the timeline is affected. Either way, you correct the issues and submit a revision, which sends you back through the gate. No shame in this — it's the system working.

If yes — it passes — you advance to the second gate.

---

### [SECTION 3 — Gate Two: The BIM Technical Review]

The second gate is the Hoffman BIM technical review. This one is more detailed — it's checking your model for true design compliance.

What does that mean in practice? They overlay your model against the design model and check it against everything that defines the design: the D-R-Bs, the R-F-Is, the specs, the details, the submittals, the P&I-Ds and P-F-Ds, the one-line diagrams, and more. Basically — does your model faithfully reflect every piece of design information that applies to it?

Then the second decision: does it pass the BIM review?

If no — same drill as before. Check for schedule impact, submit a Schedule Change Notice if needed, correct the issues, and submit a revision. Back through the gate.

If yes — you've cleared both gates.

---

### [SECTION 4 — Approval]

Once you pass both the C-M review and the BIM technical review, the I-F-F submittal is approved.

The final step is to submit the I-F-F on Procore — remember from part one, Procore is where the official documentation lives. That submission is the formal record that your model is approved for fabrication.

And that's the moment everything has been building toward: your coordinated, reviewed, compliant model is now cleared to become real, fabricated work.

---

### [OUTRO]

So there's the I-F-F procedure: preview it in the weekly meeting, pass the C-M review, pass the BIM technical review, and submit on Procore. Two gates, with correction loops if you stumble, and a Schedule Change Notice anytime the timeline takes a hit.

In our final lesson, we'll cover what happens when something doesn't match between the model and the real world out in the field — the model-to-field quality workflow. See you there.
